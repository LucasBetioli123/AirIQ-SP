# AirIQ SP: technical details

> Generated by `scripts/build_report.py` from the CSVs in `reports/metrics/`: no number here was typed by hand. Overview and setup in the [README](../README.md).

## 1. Results and scope

Final feature schema: **causal-spatial-aod-v2**. 11,543 station-days from 30 CETESB stations, from 2021-01-13 to 2023-04-04, in the state of São Paulo, Brazil. Grid of 0.05° (~5.5 km).

Validated on unseen stations and a later period, the regularized LightGBM with an IDW offset reached **MAE 2.98 µg/m³, RMSE 4.72 µg/m³ and R² 0.728**. The MAE gain over IDW was **4.34%**. The nominal 90% interval for the daily estimate had an empirical coverage of **90.61%**. D+1 coverage is measured separately in section 5. The MAE gain comes with a caveat, detailed in section 5.1: the model smooths episodes and catches fewer days above 25 µg/m³ than IDW and persistence. For alerts, the upper bound of the interval is the right signal.

There is no live feed: the product is a historical demonstration. A forecast issued at the end of day D uses the complete aggregates of D and produces the estimate for D and the forecast for D+1. "Today/tomorrow" in the agent use an explicit historical reference date. The CLI takes the reference date; horizon 1 means target date = reference + one day.

The public, anonymous PM2.5 archive limits the period to 2021–2023 and has gaps on roughly half of the days. The priority was to get both modules working end to end, with spatio-temporal validation, measured uncertainty and traceable answers.

## 2. Data, sources and reproduction

| Variable | Source used | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Hourly PM2.5 | CETESB via the public OpenAQ archive on S3 | Same CETESB network stations; CETESB's QUALAR portal requires registration and has no anonymous API. Daily mean only with at least 18 valid hours |
| AOD | GOES-16 ABI L2 AODF | MCD19A2 (MAIAC) is not in the Planetary Computer catalog (checked on 2026-10-30; only other MODIS products are) and GEE/LP DAAC require an account. GOES-16 is geostationary: eight daytime scans per day vs. two MODIS overpasses, so fewer days lost to clouds; DQF quality control |
| Tropospheric NO2 | Sentinel-5P TROPOMI, public COG on MEEO/S3 | Traffic/industry signal and secondary-aerosol precursor; qa ≥ 0.75 |
| Fire and FRP | GOES-16 ABI L2 FDCF | INPE's BDQueimadas aggregates, among others, GOES-16's own detections. FDCF also provides fire radiative power (FRP), used to weight fires, and keeps the same sensor as AOD |
| Weather | ERA5, public NCAR mirror on S3 | Wind, humidity, rain, temperature, clouds and boundary layer, without a CDS key |
| Land use and terrain | ESA WorldCover 2021 and Copernicus DEM | Public tiles with partial reads |
| State boundary and municipalities | IBGE, mirrored on GitHub | Same territorial boundaries, without depending on portals that block automated access |

The collectors live in `src/aq/data/`; they use partial HTTP reads of NetCDF/HDF5 and COG. `python -m src.aq.pipeline --full` downloads, builds the tables, benchmarks, trains and generates the evidence. `python -m src.aq.pipeline` reruns the full modeling on the ~12 MB sample without downloading raw data. For a demo in a few minutes, `python -m src.aq.pipeline --quick` trains and calibrates the production models, makes a forecast and a map, and keeps the versioned test CSVs. The sample holds the full training tables with features plus context cubes for a few date windows, enough for maps, CLI and agent.

Dates are aggregated over the local day (UTC−3). AOD/NO2 use a 0.05° cell and 3×3/9×9 neighborhoods; ERA5 uses bilinear interpolation; static layers use 1 km and a 5 km mean. The hourly timestamp of the target marks the end of the hour: 00:00 is assigned to the previous day.

### AOD gaps and causality

Spatial-only hierarchy: observed cell (level 0), 3×3 mean (1), 9×9 mean (2), missing (4). After these steps, NaN remains as a missingness pattern learned by LightGBM, together with the flag. There is no temporal filling, and no D+1 observation enters the features of D.

AOD observed in the cell for **50.8%** of rows; after spatial gap-filling, **73.9%**. AOD alone is a weak surface signal: it measures the column, which is affected by humidity, aerosol height and transport. That is why physical combinations, NO2, fire and neighboring stations are added.

An earlier version used D−1/D+1 temporal filling and the observed weather of tomorrow; both were removed to guarantee causality. `scripts/migrate_causal_sample.py` performs an idempotent migration of the stored spatial features, recomputes the physical combinations and drops future weather fields. The full download already produces the new schema. Old tables and models are rejected by a schema-identifier check.

## 3. Features and validation

| Group | Physical motivation |
|---|---|
| AOD / boundary-layer height | Approximates the relation between the column and near-surface concentration |
| AOD × (1−RH)^0.6 | Approximate correction for hygroscopic growth |
| BLH × wind speed | Ventilation and dispersion index |
| Rain on D and D−1 | Wet removal |
| Fire within 150/400 km weighted by wind direction, FRP and distance | Smoke transport, including a one-day lag |
| NO2, urbanization, vegetation and terrain | Local sources and dispersion context |
| IDW of the other stations on D | Observed regional level; the target station is excluded |
| Calendar of D and D+1 | Seasonality and the day of week known at issue time |

The point model's target is `log1p(PM2.5) − log1p(IDW)`: the model learns a local correction on top of a strong regional baseline. The whole benchmark uses the causal features.

Stations are split into five deterministic groups. Each group is held completely out of training and out of the IDW references during its test. The temporal split crosses these groups with:

- T1: train up to May 2021, calibrate in June, test July–December 2021 (dry/smoke season).
- T2: train up to March 2022, calibrate April–June, test July 2022–April 2023.

For the forecast, the **D+1 target date** sets the train, calibration and test boundaries, so the last training label never crosses the boundary. Random validation is only an optimistic reference. The metrics pool the folds; there is no untouched external test set for selecting the whole model family, so the benchmark should be read as an exploratory comparison, not a selection-bias-free estimate.

An honest reading: the model lowers MAE but **smooths episodes**. For the estimate it catches 62% of days above 25 µg/m³, versus 79% for IDW. For D+1 the gap widens: 37% versus 64% for persistence in scenario A. It raises fewer false alarms (higher precision) but misses polluted days. For alerts, the point estimate should not be used alone. The fixed rule "upper bound of the 90% interval above 25", with no tuning on the test set, catches 91% of polluted days for D+1 (scenario A), with 33% precision. In other words, the interval carries the risk signal that the point estimate loses. An alert threshold or classifier calibrated outside the test set is the natural next step.

The bands from Moderate to Hazardous follow the Ministry of Environment's technical guide. CONAMA Res. 491/2018 (Annex IV) only publishes the N1 – Good row (PM2.5 0–25 µg/m³). Res. 506/2024 (Annex II) lowered that band to 0–15 µg/m³ from 2024-07-09. The data cover 2021 to 2023, so the metrics correctly use the 491 rule. A query dated after 2024 would need the 506 band.

## 6. Maps and interfaces

`reports/figures/mapa_pm25_*` holds daily estimate/forecast maps and maps of the 90% interval width. The portable command produces a typical day, a polluted day and a smoke day; the D+1 map for 2021-08-24 is issued on 2021-08-23. CLI, map, API and the agent's tool share the same inference function.

The output includes concentration, bounds, IQAr band, reference and target dates, the legal standard in force on the target date, whether it is exceeded, and AOD/IDW diagnostics. The distance to the nearest station is computed from real latitude/longitude pairs. The production model is trained on all data for the demo: historical maps may be in-sample and do not replace the fold metrics.

## 7. RAG agent

Seven public Brazilian federal documents, in Portuguese: CONAMA Res. 491/2018, 506/2024, 03/1990, 05/1989; Law 6.938/1981; Decree 99.274/1990; Law 9.605/1998. Sources and partial repeals are in `configs/normas.yaml`. It does not aim to cover all environmental law in force.

The parser preserves chapter, article, paragraph, item, sub-item and annex. The preferred unit is the article; long articles are split with hierarchy metadata. Tables are turned into rows with the column name next to each value, so PI-1, PI-2 and PF are never confused. Retrieval combines BM25 and Granite-278M embeddings with RRF; a Qwen3-0.6B reranker reorders the candidates and provides the score used for refusals. The local Qwen3-4B LLM plans the queries, receives passages and tool outputs, answers with references and goes through numeric validation. The PM2.5 tool returns the value, the interval and precomputed comparisons; the standard-in-force tool provides the schedule and the legal evidence. Out-of-scope questions can be refused by the threshold or by the model. Every run logs the question, retrieval, calls, answer, tokens, latency and cost; the local cost is zero.

Incomplete downloads never replace the base; ingestion atomically writes only the complete set and preserves the versioned sample. Retrieval also detects an incomplete processed base and falls back to the complete sample. `.env` is loaded without executing code and without overriding variables already exported.

### Evaluation, answer keys and metric limits

36 questions written by hand and checked against the text of the regulations: 17 facts, 6 multi-passage, 5 temporal validity, 2 with a tool and 6 unanswerable from the base. Citation precision checks the exact **provision**, not just the document. For tool questions, place, target date, horizon, value and both interval bounds are required; when the question asks for a band, the returned band is required instead of accepting any name. The citation of the standard is checked separately; the semantics of the comparison depends on the source/judge analysis, not only on the presence of numbers.

## 8. Priorities, discarded options and next steps

- MAIAC/QUALAR/CDS/INPE were replaced by public, anonymous sources, keeping the physical nature of the signals (rationale per source in section 2).
- The model was chosen by MAE. By the polluted-day detection criterion, IDW and persistence are better (section 5.1). The criterion was not changed after seeing the test set; the conflict is documented and the interval is used for alerts.
- Gap-filling with D+1 AOD and the observed weather of D+1 were removed to respect the issue horizon.
- An episode-trend feature for D+1 (PM2.5 and IDW of D−1, known at the end of D) was tested and discarded: it worsened MAE (A: 4.05 → 4.14; B: 4.47 → 4.54) and detection above 25 µg/m³. The experiment exactly reproduces the published baselines (`scripts/exp_tendencia_d1.py`, `logs/exp_tendencia_d1.txt`).
- Absolute targets, neural networks and more complex variants remain in the benchmark; the chosen model learns the correction over IDW. Small or negative gains in some slices are reported.
- No live daily feed or ERA5 latency simulation was implemented. The historical demo is not presented as current monitoring.
- There is no evaluation in other states, no coverage guarantee at the extremes and no complete São Paulo state legislation in the base.
- IQAr bands follow 491/2018 for every date. Correct for 2021–2023, but outdated after 506/2024 (Good ≤ 15 µg/m³).
- Next steps: more stations and years, weather data available at issue time, optional numerical weather prediction, weighting of extremes/plume height, an alert threshold calibrated outside the test set, IQAr bands by date of validity, an external RAG test set and independent calibration of the thresholds.
